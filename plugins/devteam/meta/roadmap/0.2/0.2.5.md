## 2. Decisions

L-8 applies. Local:

- **L-5.1 — the outgoing manager never spawns its successor.** *Why:* a
  session cannot start an interactive successor, and a headless successor
  cannot reach a `terminal` client; the successor is started by whoever
  owns the channel — a person, or a scheduler the operator runs. The
  outgoing side's whole job is to *become safely replaceable* and say so.
- **L-5.2 — the handoff is a live exchange when the outgoing session is
  reachable, and a reconstruction when it is not**, exactly as `resume` §0
  already says; this subcycle adds the file that tells the incoming session
  which case it is in. *Why:* the owner's stated practice, and the
  measurement it yields — every question asked is a defect in the record.
- **L-5.3 — the periodic refresh (CONSOLIDATION 6) is the small set only:**
  width, the pinned environment, the live claims, the protected paths, the
  priority order, the client channel — re-read from the board header on
  every report. Everything else is re-read at the decision it governs.
  *Why:* re-reading a 34 KB charter on a timer is not affordable and not
  what makes the difference; a decision made with the governing text one
  screen above it is.

## 3. The work

### 3.1 The trigger and the outgoing side — `run` §7 and a new §7b

After a checkpoint is filed and committed (`ON-COURSE` or otherwise):

1. write `devteam/.run/session/handoff-ready`:
   ```
   session <id> checkpoint C-n at <iso>
   claims-in-flight: <T-n list or none>
   sandboxes-open: <ids or none>
   ```
2. append to `RECORD.md`: `rotation due (C-n): handoff-ready written; this
   session continues until the successor takes the lock`
3. tell the client, by the charter's channel: *"Rotation is due. Start a
   fresh session in this directory and run `/devteam:resume`; keep this one
   open until it reports it holds the lock."* For channel `none`, the line
   goes into the record and the checkpoint only; for `session <name>`, it
   is sent there.
4. **keep working.** Nothing stops; the loop continues until the successor
   takes the lock. Stopping would idle the pipeline on a human's schedule,
   which P-28 forbids for a single task and forbids doubly for all of them.

### 3.2 The incoming side — `resume` §0, extended

When `handoff-ready` exists and names another session:

1. `ListAgents` — is that session listed? If yes, message it per §0 as
   written (drive; ask only what the files could not say). If 0.2.0 §3.5
   found that a session whose turn has ended is **not** reachable, the skill
   says so in one sentence and proceeds to reconstruction; the outgoing
   session's §3.1 step 4 then matters more, because "keep working" is what
   keeps it reachable.
2. take the lock: board writer line, `.run/session/manager`, commit
   `board: writer <new id> (rotation from <old id>, C-n)`; write
   `writer handoff: <old> → <new>` in `RECORD.md`.
3. message the outgoing session: *"I hold the lock as of <commit>. Finish
   nothing further; end your turn."* — and the outgoing session's `run` §2
   says what it does on receiving that: one last record line, then it ends
   its turn and takes no further action on that project. **The guard
   enforces the rest**: its `devteam/` writes are refused from that commit
   on (P-13), which is the control for this whole mechanism.
4. remove `handoff-ready` (the manager may remove an untracked file it
   created — `run` §9b — and this one was created by its predecessor for
   exactly this reader; say so in the record line).
5. log every question that had to be asked as `record defect: the incoming
   session had to ask <what>` — one line each, under the day's entry. That
   list is the measurement.

### 3.3 The measurement that tunes the cadence

Two numbers per rotation, both from commands, recorded at the next
checkpoint under a `## Rotation` heading:

- **manager tokens since the last rotation** — the sum of the harness's
  `budget.json` figures is the *workers'* cost; the manager's own reading is
  the CLI's own usage for the session, which is available on this machine
  from the session's transcript metadata (`REASONED` — find where the
  harness records per-session usage and cite the path; if there is no
  observable, say so and count report bytes handled instead, which is
  countable: `wc -c` over the task files' execution records appended since)
- **questions asked at the handoff** — the count of `record defect` lines

The cadence is "every checkpoint" until three rotations exist; then the
checkpoint skill's §4 ("is the plan still right?") gains one question: is
the rotation cadence right, given those two numbers.

### The waking half was measured, and the mechanism was built not to need it

0.2.0 §3.5 measured half of `SendMessage` to a session whose turn has ended —
addressing holds — and deliberately did not run the waking half, because every
peer on the machine was one of the owner's live working sessions and a probe
writes into context nobody asked to disturb.

**It has now been run three ways, and by coordination rather than by probe:**
three sessions were working this repository at once and had to talk to each
other about who was writing where, so nothing was disturbed that was not
already involved. A session whose turn had ended woke and replied; two idle
sessions that had never been prompted also replied; a send to an exited session
returned `ENOENT ... the peer process may have restarted`, and `ListAgents`
lists the live-but-idle one and not the exited one.

**And the mechanism was built not to depend on any of it.** `run` §7b has the
outgoing manager announce while it is mid-turn — nothing to wake — and `resume`
§0 has the incoming session drive the asking. **The direction of the first
message and the direction of the questioning are two different things**, and
conflating them is what made rotation appear to need a measurement it did not
need. Three data points, one machine, one day: safe-because-measured is weaker
than not-depended-on, and a harness change retires all three. The measurement
now justifies the fallback. 0.2.0 §3.5's block is corrected in place, because
a reader who checks it would otherwise find "not run" and re-derive the hedge
that had just been deleted.

### Two things the plan asked for that its own mechanism forbids

**§3.2 asked the outgoing manager to write `RECORD.md` after losing the lock.**
Step 3: *"one last record line, then it ends its turn."* `RECORD.md` is inside
`devteam/`, so from the successor's commit that write is refused — by exactly
the rule the same step names as *"the control for this whole mechanism"*. This
is not a typo. **It is the plan not having simulated its own rule**, and it was
written into `run` §2 verbatim before the guard's message made it obvious.

Resolved by moving the write rather than exempting it: the successor records
the handoff from the side that still holds the lock, which is why `resume` §0
carries `writer handoff: <old> → <new>` and `run` §2 now says the predecessor
writes nothing at all.

**§3.6 asked for a `handoff-ready` parser that nothing needs.** Once the file
is a pointer, `resume` reads it with `cat`. The parser survives only where a
*program* must branch on it — and there the plan had it backwards: the control
it specified for `reinject.py` ("the handoff line appears when the file names
another session and not otherwise") is only satisfiable if `reinject` reads the
file, which the plan's own §3.5 described as a static line. The line is
conditional; that is where the grammar lives; and no new `PERMISSIONS.md` grant
was needed, which matters because that is a client-visible surface.

### Guidance attached to a refusal inherits the refusal's blast radius

**The sharpest finding, and it is a new shape.** `guard.py`'s refusal for a
`devteam/` write by a session that does not hold the lock ended:

> *"If that session is gone, take the lock: set the `**Writer.**` line to this
> session's id ... and record the takeover in RECORD.md."*

Correct for the case its author had in mind — a crashed predecessor. **After a
rotation it is an instruction to commit the two-writer failure P-13 exists to
prevent**, because the session holding the lock is the successor and it is not
gone. A manager following the refusal's own remedy would take the lock back
from the session that had just legitimately taken it.

This is the rule-pairs shape — two things that cannot both hold — but located
where nobody has been looking for it: **not in two rules, in the remedy
attached to one.** A refusal is written once, by someone imagining one cause,
and then fires for every cause. Its guidance is as broad as the condition that
triggers it, and nothing anywhere diffs the two.

Fixed by reading `handoff-ready` **on the refusal path only** — never on the
allow path, so the ordinary case pays nothing — and giving a replaced manager a
message that names the file, says it has been replaced, and tells it not to
take the lock back. The generic recipe is unchanged for every other case, with
a false-positive twin asserting it still fires.

### Two readers of one format, and why the control must not assert they agree

`reinject.py` and `guard.py` both parse `handoff-ready`, and **neither may
import the other**: a hook that fails to import is a hook that breaks every
tool call, which is why both are stdlib-only and standalone. So the duplication
is deliberate and the drift is what needs controlling.

The control asserts they extract **the same session id**. It deliberately does
*not* assert equal verdicts, because they correctly disagree on one input: a
file naming a session but carrying no checkpoint is `malformed` to `reinject`
and still a rotation to the guard, and **both are right** — they are answering
different questions over the same two lines. Demanding identical verdicts to
prove non-drift would have manufactured a false equivalence *inside the control
written to prevent drift*, which is P-35b at one more remove than this project
has caught it before.

- **the handoff's session id matched by substring instead of by exact token.**

Both now have cases; both mutations fail exactly those cases and nothing else.
The general shape, and it is the same one this cycle keeps producing: *a
control written from the mechanism's intended shape tests the intended shape.*
Only a mutation of the mechanism asks what else would pass.

### Cost

**Opening reading, and it is not a zero point.** Requested from the owner
before any work began; it arrived after the mechanism was built, so it is
stamped with when it was actually taken rather than presented as a baseline.

```
$ python3 plugins/devteam/scripts/session_cost.py     # MEASURED
requests 106 · uncached input 212 · output 111,094 · cache read 15,842,241
cache write 258,643 · total processed 16,212,190 · cache hit share 98.4%
```

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Gauge, current week, all models | **91% used**, resets **9 Sep, 7:59pm** | owner's `/usage`, **read** |
| Boost | **+50% weekly, active, ends 13 Sep** | owner's `/usage` |
| Session cost at the moment of that reading | **$12.28** over **101 requests** | owner's `/usage` |
| Taken at | ~101 requests into 0.2.5, **not at zero** | this session |

**The straddle is 9 September, not the 13th, and an earlier draft of this
section had it wrong.** The reasoning that produced the error is worth keeping
because it is the ordinary one: the boost's end date was the salient number, so
it became the boundary, and nobody looked at when the window itself turns over.
The weekly window **resets on 9 September, four days before the boost expires**.
So:

- **0.2.5 closing before 9 Sep, 7:59pm** — opening and closing readings are in
  the same boosted window, and the row is comparable to 0.2.0–0.2.4.
- **0.2.5 running past it** — the readings are of **two different windows** and
  the row is not comparable. The cause is the *reset*, not the boost.
- **The boost expiry falls inside the NEXT window (9–16 Sep)**, so it is
  0.2.6's problem or 0.2.8's, and whichever subcycle owns that window should be
  told before it writes a row.

**One reading that has not moved is not one reading.** 0.2.4 closed at `91%`
and this opens at `91%`, with `$12.28` and 16.2 M tokens processed in between.
A whole-percent gauge cannot distinguish "this cost under a point" from "this
cost nothing", and 0.2.4 already recorded being caught by exactly that — a
`0` that was a reading taken too early. The honest statement is **under one
point, direction unknown**, and it stays that way until a closing reading moves
or does not.

**And the scheduling fact, which is the only part that governs a decision:**
91% used with the window resetting on 9 September. What remains of 0.2.5 —
§4's live proof across two dedicated sessions — is small, but it is not free,
and it is the owner's call whether it runs before the reset or after it.

Two standing rules were followed rather than rediscovered, both from §8: **read
the gauge, never derive it**, and **the dollars are an API-equivalent size
measure between sessions, not what the owner pays.**
